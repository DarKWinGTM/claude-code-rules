# Mechanism-Backed Research and Remedy Selection

Use this procedure when new evidence could change a proposal's feasibility, criteria or architecture. It supports the parent diagnosis Skill; ordinary known repairs do not need a research ceremony.

## Frame the Decision

1. State the desired outcome and binding constraints. Keep the user's goal separate from an exploratory implementation suggestion.
2. Identify the pivotal assumption: which actual platform capability or control must reach which consumer to produce the outcome? Trace that link before endorsing dependent architecture. A self-defined component contract or fake capability does not establish that the real platform provides it.
3. Record the current path and its owner. Compare replacement/addition with repair, reconnection or using an already available path. Unknown implementation is not proof that capability is missing.

## Gather Discriminating Evidence

Choose the smallest eligible source or authorized experiment that could reject the assumption: the actual implementation, an official contract, a representative response or a reversible local probe. Search current external documentation when the decision depends on drift-prone capability, compatibility or provider terms. Prefer direct primary authority; expose meaningful conflicts instead of averaging unequal sources.

Keep supplied evidence, local observation, inference and unresolved details distinct. A bounded search without precedent proves neither impossibility nor novelty. Missing access narrows the claim; it does not authorize guessed private routes, credential collection or another transport. Bound input and output before processing large evidence.

## Compare Realistic Alternatives

Use criteria tied to the outcome: functionality, control boundary, security/privacy, state/recovery behavior, operational cost and maintenance. Compare only alternatives that could change the decision, including the simpler complete route. Do not add a numerical scoring system or fixed option count when a direct comparison is sufficient.

When evidence invalidates a premise, withdraw dependent conclusions, preserve accepted goals and genuine earlier proof, and revise only affected criteria and options. Domain-specific examples are evidence of their mechanism, not universal policy.

## Evaluate Optimizations Separately

Correctness and performance are different claims. Before timing a candidate, compare observable behavior, data ownership, side-effect order and failure/recovery semantics against the unchanged control. Include relevant aliasing, concurrency or interruption boundaries only where they can change equivalence. A catch/rebuild after an irreversible effect may not restore the original ordering.

A useful paired benchmark holds workload, inputs, environment and measurement boundary comparable, checks correctness, and records substitutions and variance. A synthetic fixture, one run, a smaller file or fewer allocations alone does not establish real-workload speed, resource containment or stability. If behavior diverges, stop the performance endorsement and resolve the contract first.

## Synthesize and Stop

Distinguish a method/tool blocker from a blocker of the goal. Missing an optional investigation tool is not evidence of goal infeasibility; continue supported source/contract conclusions and realistic alternatives without installing unrelated machinery. If a required capability or binding constraint is contradicted, limit or reject that method at the earned scope and state what remains unresolved.

Give the supported answer first, then its conditions, decisive evidence and any next discriminator that could change it. Recommend the best-supported route with its trade-off, implementation owner and matching proof. Mark untested assumptions and the exact next discriminator. Source/local evidence may support a conditional design without a mandatory live experiment. An explicitly selected live terminal gate stays pending until its prerequisite is available; optional stronger evidence does not reopen an independently satisfied gate.

Research results do not approve new infrastructure, publication, deployment or consequential experiments. Hand any material scope/authority choice back to its existing owner; stop when more research would not change the selected decision.
